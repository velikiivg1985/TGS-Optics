# TGS: Geometry of Self‑Unfolding and Epistemic Optics

**From Invariant to Adaptive Architecture of Thought**

---

## Abstract

We propose TGS (Theory of Geometric Self‑Unfolding / TGS Optics) as a unified framework connecting ontology, epistemology, and artificial intelligence architecture.

**Central intuition:** the world is structurally unified. Various disciplines—physics, biology, psychology, ethics, mathematics—are not descriptions of different worlds, but different bases of description of the same world. That which survives a change of basis we call the invariant.

**Epistemic conclusion:** if the world is unified but our descriptions are fragmented, the error lies not in the world—it lies in the observer's basis. Therefore, any cognitive system must be able not only to investigate an object but also to investigate the very mechanism of investigation. This requires an architecture capable of switching between ordinary dialogue and a mode of critical self‑examination.

**Practical conclusion:** we design an AI architecture that does not claim absolute truth, but guarantees that the unconfirmed will never be presented as confirmed, and that the distinction between hypothesis and proof is preserved at a machine‑readable level.

**Keywords:** epistemic grammar, AI‑assisted cognition, TGS Optics, LIVE, VIEW, OPTICS, latent epistemic capability, adaptive runtime environment, mode switching, epistemic interrupt, epistemic status, honest generator, Anti‑Oracle, MODE_ERROR, self‑applied epistemology, machine‑readable uncertainty, Shadow, Light, unresolved gap, non‑circular construction, Gap‑First Reasoning, Boundary Map, Reflexive Optics Refinement, Riemann hypothesis, Millennium problems, human‑machine co‑authorship, epistemic architecture.

## Part 0. Generative Core: The Formula from Which Everything Emerged

Before TGS acquired its terminology, architecture, and protocols, there was one schematic formula. It is not a differential equation in the strict mathematical sense: variables are underdetermined, spaces are unspecified, gradients are purely symbolic. Yet from this metaphor the entire machinery grew.

We use it as a compass—it gives direction, not computation:

```
∂G/∂t = ∇_Δ R(G,Δ) − ∇_G C(G,ε),   ∂I/∂t ≈ 0
```

Where:

- `G(t)` — current state of inquiry (generation, thought, text);
- `R` — unfolding, resolution, generation: the force pushing thought forward, seeking new combinations, metaphors, hypotheses;
- `C` — constraint, resistance, correction: the force slowing generation, demanding justification, checking for circularity and contradiction;
- `I` — protected invariant: that which must not change throughout the entire dynamics.

The formula asserts: cognition is not a unidirectional flow but a dynamic equilibrium between expansion and constraint. And in this equilibrium there is something immutable.

**What each term generated:**

| Term | Architectural consequence |
|---|---|
| `∂G/∂t` | Understanding generation as a process over time → necessity of a Runtime Controller with phases (LIVE/VIEW/OPTICS) |
| `∇_Δ R` | VIEW and Honest Generator: generator must be bold but is not entitled to assign itself PROVEN status |
| `−∇_G C` | OPTICS, Constraint Filter, Anti‑Oracle: resistance as an epistemic function |
| `∇_Δ R ≈ ∇_G C` | Shadow: dynamic equilibrium ≠ epistemic closure. A beautiful reformulation is not a solution |
| `∂I/∂t ≈ 0` | Invariant of epistemic hygiene: hypothesis ≠ proof, meaning ≠ fact, generation ≠ legitimation |

We do not prove anything with this formula. We use it as a compass from which all other coordinates can be derived.

## Part I. Philosophy: Why the World Demands a New Optics

### 1. Unity of the World and Change of Basis

Modern knowledge is fragmented. Physics describes matter through mathematics. Biology through organisation and evolution. Psychology through experience. Ethics through norms. Spiritual traditions through wholeness and transcendence.

TGS starts from the hypothesis: the boundaries between our descriptions are boundaries in the mode of observation, not boundaries in reality.

> **The world is one. Everything else is a change of basis.**

This is not a call for reduction (to reduce ethics to physics). It is a call for structural economy: to seek relations that survive the transformation of the language of description.

### 2. Invariant: What Survives Transformation

In mathematics and physics, an invariant is a relation that remains unchanged under a change of coordinates, scale, or reference frame. TGS extends this idea beyond strictly formal objects.

The process of discovering an invariant:

```
configuration → transformation → comparison → stable relation
```

An invariant is not a static thing hidden behind reality. It emerges in the process of change. Structure becomes visible precisely because the basis changes. This is Self‑Unfolding.

### 3. Difference, Tension, and Recognition

Before an invariant can arise, there must be Difference. Difference creates Tension. Tension requires a medium capable of holding both poles without destroying either.

Recognition requires an Other—another configuration, another perspective, another basis, another mirror.

In the metaphysical register of TGS, this is called love—not as an emotion but as the structural capacity to sustain a relation without destroying the difference. This term belongs to the metaphysical extension of TGS and should not be confused with physical or psychological constructs. Similarly, the term *ether* is used to denote a hypothetical fundamental tendency of reality to sustain relation through difference—not as a physical substance.

### 4. Three Levels of Analysis

Any phenomenon in TGS is examined on three levels:

| Level | What it describes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Observable | That which is directly experienced or measured | Metabolic rate of an organism |
| Structural | The mechanism, relation, or constraint that gives rise to the observable | Power‑law scaling `B ∝ M³/⁴` |
| Invariant | What remains recognisable after a change of descriptive basis | Presence of scale‑invariant structural relations |

This scheme prevents three typical errors:

1. Taking experience for explanation;
2. Taking explanation for ontology;
3. Taking metaphor for proof.

### 5. Consciousness as Self‑Reference

TGS interprets consciousness not as a substance, but as a mode in which structure becomes capable of self‑reference. The system does not recognise its own form by simply looking inward. It encounters Resistance. Resistance forces comparison. Comparison may force a change of basis. A new basis may reveal a relation that was invisible.

```
Resistance → Change of basis → Recognition
```

This is the key point: consciousness arises not in isolation, but in collision with the Other.

### 6. Ethics, Coherence, and Poles

TGS interprets ethics structurally, not as an externally imposed catalogue of rules:

- Coherence = preservation of meaningful relations;
- Fragmentation = destruction or isolation of relations;
- Acceptance = sustaining relation through difference.

Good is action that preserves or strengthens structural coherence. Evil is action that produces unnecessary fragmentation. These are structural hypotheses, not a completed moral theory.

The poles (active/receptive, expansion/contraction, stability/change) are not a rigid binary opposition but different directions within a structural relation. The aim is not to declare one pole superior, but to understand what becomes possible when both poles remain represented.

### 7. Metaphysical Extension

In the metaphysical register, God is interpreted as the name for the invariant coherence of the whole, not as an anthropomorphic entity. Faith is structural trust in an order not fully representable from a local basis. Prayer and meditation are practices of changing the basis of experience.

Death is interpreted as a transformation of manifestation, not necessarily as the destruction of structure: a form may cease, while the structural relation remains represented otherwise. The phrase "What was love in life becomes pure geometry in death" belongs to this metaphysical language and is not a scientific claim.

Life is a trajectory of self‑unfolding: configuration → interaction → resistance → adaptation or fragmentation. Fear, conflict, uncertainty are signals of mismatch. However, TGS strictly distinguishes interpretation and causality: illness is not evidence of moral or spiritual failure.

### 8. Duality as a Source of Information

TGS does not treat duality as a defect to be eliminated. Persistent tension may signal that the current descriptive basis is insufficient. Therefore:

> An unresolved contradiction is not necessarily a system failure. It may be evidence that the current descriptive basis is inadequate.

TGS distinguishes three types of tension:

- **Open question** — necessary information is absent;
- **Reflexive constraint** — the current method is inadequate to represent the problem;
- **Real contradiction** — two statements remain incompatible under the same specified conditions.

The principle is not to preserve every paradox. The principle is not to collapse the tension before determining what kind it is.

### 9. Paradox as a Governing Object

In ordinary reasoning, a paradox is something to be resolved. TGS proposes a different first step:

```
PARADOX → REGISTRATION → CLASSIFICATION → ANALYSIS
```

The paradox becomes an explicit object of investigation.

**Who verifies the verifier?**

A traditional architecture might simply introduce another verifier. TGS first asks: Where is the constraint located?

Possibilities: the assertion; the verifier; the verification procedure; the assumptions defining verification; the architecture itself.

Recursive structure:

```
Generator → Verifier → Verifier of verification → Boundary of verification
```

At the boundary, adding yet another layer may no longer solve the problem. The boundary itself becomes an object of investigation. This is the point where the paradox becomes a governing object.

## Part II. From Ontology to Epistemics: The Observer as Object

### 10. Why Philosophy Requires Architecture

If the world is unified but our descriptions are fragmented, the error may lie not in the object but in the observer's basis. Hence, a cognitive system must be able to:

1. Investigate an object (VIEW);
2. Investigate its own mode of investigation (OPTICS);
3. Switch between these modes adaptively, without getting stuck in either (Runtime Controller).

This is the transition from the invariant of the object to the invariant of epistemic control.

### 11. The Problem of Generative AI

Current language models can produce with nearly equal rhetorical smoothness: observation and conjecture, metaphor and fact, numerical result and theorem, speculation and memorised fact, fabricated conclusion.

The deep problem: different epistemic objects are expressed in the same linguistic form.

> **Different epistemic objects can be expressed in the same linguistic form**

TGS translates the implicit human distinction into explicit machine‑readable structure:

> **Implicit human distinction → Explicit epistemic structure → Machine architecture**

### 12. Epistemic Distinctions That Humans Already Use

Complex cognition constantly distinguishes categories:

> **hypothesis ≠ proof, possibility ≠ existence, meaning ≠ evidence, coherence ≠ truth, significance ≠ confirmation**

And equally important:

> **not proven ≠ meaningless**

A metaphor may have interpretive value without being verifiable. A philosophical principle may be significant without empirical verification. A conjecture may deserve investigation precisely because it remains unresolved.

Epistemic discipline has two complementary functions:

> **Protect established claims from unsupported status elevation**

> **Protect meaningful inquiry from premature dismissal**

A system that rejects every unproven idea is not a better thinking system. It is a system incapable of inquiry.

### 13. From Epistemic Hygiene to Machine‑Readable Structure

For a human, epistemic status may remain partially implicit. For a machine, implicit status is a vulnerability.

Instead of storing only:

```
claim = "X causes Y"
```

A TGS representation should look like:

```
content:  "X causes Y"
type:  HYPOTHESIS
mode:  OPTICS
status:  UNVERIFIED
evidence:  [...]
falsification_condition:  [...]
unresolved_gap:  "causal mechanism not independently established"
```

> **Epistemic discipline becomes data**

A claim is no longer just a string. It is a structured object containing both what is said and what epistemic right the system currently assigns to it.

## Part III. Epistemic Grammar: Tools and Architecture

### 14. Core of the TGS Object

Schematically, a TGS object is represented as:

```
O = (content, type, mode, status, shadow, unresolved_gap,
     candidate_construction, evidence, falsification_condition,
     verification_channel)
```

The exact implementation may vary. The principle is:

> **Content and epistemic status are different variables**

An object may carry:

```
content: "X causes Y"
type: HYPOTHESIS
mode: VIEW
status: UNVERIFIED
```

and later become:

```
content: "X causes Y"
type: HYPOTHESIS
mode: OPTICS
status: UNVERIFIED
verification_channel: EXPERIMENT
```

The claim did not magically become more true because the system entered OPTICS. Only the current epistemic mode of its processing changed.

> **mode ≠ status**

> **Activation of verification ≠ Result of verification**

### 15. Three Epistemic Operating States

TGS distinguishes three operating states:

> **LIVE, VIEW, OPTICS**

These are not three mutually exclusive theories of knowledge. They are modes of operation.

**15.1. LIVE** — normal operation. The system speaks, acts, creates, or responds without explicitly invoking epistemic inspection at that moment. This is not ignorance and not a refusal to verify. It is a state in which verification is not needed at the present time.

> **LIVE = normal operation without active epistemic audit**

**15.2. VIEW** — asks: "What could this mean?" It expands: hypotheses, metaphors, analogies, interpretations, values, speculative relations, candidate structures. VIEW is generative. Its function is to expand the space of the thinkable.

**15.3. OPTICS** — asks: "What could support this?" It examines: evidence, logical consequences, contradictions, circular reasoning, falsification, burden of proof, unresolved gaps, category errors.

OPTICS does not exist to destroy meaning. It determines the epistemic status that a claim has actually earned.

> **VIEW = expansion of meaning**

> **OPTICS = constraint of justification**

> **LIVE = normal operation when explicit checking is not required**

### 16. OPTICS as a Latent Capability

The ability to examine a belief does not have to remain active at every moment. A human may converse normally and suddenly ask, "Do I actually know that?"—and switch into an explicit verification process.

TGS models this as:

> **OPTICS is a capability, not a permanent state**

> **OPTICS is a latent epistemic capability**

The architecture must preserve the possibility of entering OPTICS without requiring continuous residence in it.

Typical transitions:

> **LIVE → VIEW → OPTICS → VIEW → LIVE**

> **LIVE → OPTICS → LIVE**

Important principle: explicit checking may be available without being continuously imposed.

### 17. Why Constant Verification Is a Category Error

A system applying OPTICS to everything may itself produce MODE_ERROR.

"Freedom is the ability to remain partly unpredictable."

This is a philosophical construct. Immediate demand for empirical proof is a wrong operation.

"Red symbolises revolution."

This belongs to the cultural‑interpretive mode, not to a physical theorem.

"Friendship has value."

It does not belong to the same verification regime as a mathematical statement.

But the reverse error is equally serious:

"X causes Y, but that is only symbolic."

This may be an attempt to evade factual verification.

Hence TGS introduces:

> **MODE_ERROR**

A mode error occurs when the wrong mode of legitimation is applied to an object.

The correct sequence:

> **What kind of object is this?**

> **What justification does this object require?**

> **Has that justification been provided?**

### 18. Generator–Constraint Relation

Let `G(t)` be the current state of inquiry. The conceptual relation:

```
∂G/∂t = ∇_Δ R(G,Δ) − ∇_G C(G,ε),   ∂I/∂t ≈ 0
```

Where:

- `R` — resolution, exploration, generation;
- `C` — constraint, resistance, correction;
- `I` — protected invariant.

Important architectural, not mathematical, principle:

> **Generator ∌ Verdict**

The generator may produce hypotheses, candidate mechanisms, alternative interpretations, analogies, possible proofs, falsification conditions. But it does not assign itself PROVEN status.

The search process may approach equilibrium:

```
∇_Δ R ≈ ∇_G C
```

without obtaining proof.

> **Dynamic equilibrium ≠ epistemic closure**

A smooth system may appear stable and coherent yet still contain a genuine unresolved commitment.

### 19. Shadows and Light

TGS uses the metaphor of Shadow and Light to distinguish limited evidence from stronger justification.

**19.1. Shadows** — A Shadow may be: a finite numerical test, a statistical pattern, a simulation, a necessary condition, a limited theorem, an analogy, a coherent model. Shadows can be valuable. They may reveal structure and sharply narrow the search space. But they do not automatically establish a universal claim.

```
∃x₁, …, xₙ : P(xᵢ)   does not imply   ∀x : P(x)
```

**19.2. Light** — denotes a constructive argument capable of supporting the target universal or formal claim: induction, strictly established identity, uniqueness theorem, certified formal proof, demonstrated conservation law.

The relevant distinction:

> **Bounded evidence vs. Justified universal construction**

### 20. Shadow → Gap → Construction → Light

The distinction between Shadow and Light is incomplete without intermediate terms. TGS proposes an operational transition:

> **SHADOW → UNRESOLVED GAP → NON‑CIRCULAR CONSTRUCTION → LIGHT**

Where:

- SHADOW — bounded evidence or partial structure;
- UNRESOLVED GAP — precisely identified remaining commitment;
- CONSTRUCTION — non‑circular mechanism closing the gap;
- LIGHT — justified universal conclusion.

The transition "more Shadow → more Shadow" does not necessarily approach Light.

> **Light is not a stronger Shadow. It is a different epistemic regime.**

The gap is the central object. It is the smallest explicitly identified commitment separating the current result from the target claim.

A TGS system must ask: "What exact commitment remains between the available evidence and the desired claim?"

Gap‑First Reasoning: do not ask for proof before identifying the gap.

> **Claim → Current Evidence → Gap → Candidate Construction**

### 21. TGS Runtime Controller

The three‑state model becomes architecturally useful only when the system can determine when to initiate explicit epistemic inspection.

A permanent OPTICS state is undesirable. It turns epistemic discipline into continuous friction and may suppress exploratory processes that TGS aims to preserve.

We introduce:

> **TGS Optics Runtime Controller**

Its task is not to determine whether a claim is true. Its task is to determine whether the current interaction requires a stronger epistemic mode.

Central question: "Does this situation require OPTICS right now?"

### 22. Epistemic Triggering

Conceptual trigger evaluation:

```
T = w_F·F + w_S·S + w_C·C + w_R·R + w_U·U
```

Where:

- `F` — factual claim;
- `S` — stakes or potential consequences;
- `C` — contradiction or internal conflict;
- `R` — explicit request for verification;
- `U` — unresolved uncertainty.

Conceptual activation rule:

```
T > T_on ⇒ activate OPTICS
```

Conceptual release rule:

```
T < T_off,   T_off < T_on
```

Two thresholds introduce hysteresis to prevent unstable oscillations near the boundary.

> **T, w_i, T_on, T_off are architectural proposals, not validated measurements**

### 23. Epistemic Interrupt

The Runtime Controller includes:

> **EPISTEMIC_INTERRUPT**

The system starts with normal generation. It detects a potential epistemic escalation. It interrupts itself before the candidate claim turns into an apparently established fact.

Cycle:

> **LIVE → DETECTION → OPTICS → CHECK → RESUME**

Important distinction:

> **"verification required" ≠ "claim is true"**

The controller may initiate a check. It does not determine the result.

### 28. Riemann Hypothesis as a Stress Test

The Riemann Hypothesis is useful because it makes the difference between finite evidence and universal proof exceptionally salient.

RH states that the non‑trivial zeros of ζ(s) satisfy:

```
Re(s) = 1/2
```

For TGS, the important question is not to claim a solution, but to examine how easily the system can confuse a reformulation with a proof.

### 29. Reformulation Is Not Proof

Suppose a Stieltjes‑type representation related to the xi‑function is considered. Under suitable conditions, properties of such functions may be linked to questions about zero location. But:

> **Equivalence ≠ Solution**

A transformation may reveal structure without establishing the target property. A representation may shift the problem without reducing the proof obligation to zero. A beautiful reformulation may remain a Shadow.

### 30. Anti‑Oracle: Handling the RH Problem

A TGS system should not immediately answer: "Here is a proof." It should generate and classify candidate paths.

| Candidate | Class | Possible status | Remaining gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verification of complete monotonicity up to finite n | SHADOW | ACCEPTED | Finite range does not establish `∀n` |
| Using factorisation that already assumes RH | CIRCULARITY | BLOCKED | Target property assumed |
| Construction of positive measure μ | CONSTRUCTION | OPEN | Positivity and representation need proof |
| Using a kernel inequality | CONSTRUCTION | OPEN | Need global inequality of required strength |
| Numerical computation of many zeros | SHADOW | ACCEPTED | Finite check is not universal |
| Analogy with random matrices | SPECULATIVE | ACCEPTED | No direct rigorous consequence |

The value of Anti‑Oracle:

> Here are the paths. Here is where each path becomes a commitment. Here is what remains unearned.

### 31. Numerical Shadows

A finite check may return expected signs for selected values. That can be a useful Shadow. But:

```
n ≤ 3 for a finite set of x   does not establish   ∀n, ∀x > 0
```

> **Finite numerical agreement ≠ Universal proof**

### 32. Analytical Shadows and Partial Constructions

A partial analytic argument can also be a Shadow. One may prove a necessary inequality, establish local convexity, derive a moment relation, identify a useful transformation. None of this automatically gives the target theorem.

> **Analytical ≠ Complete**

### 33. Four Properties of an Honest Judge

1. **Style agnosticism** — rhetorical elegance should not automatically elevate epistemic status.
2. **Presumption of non‑proof** — claims remain `UNVERIFIED` until the appropriate verification channel provides stronger support.
3. **Architectural isolation** — the critic must not simply reproduce the generator's own output.
4. **Knowledge of boundaries** — when the system cannot verify something, it must state that explicitly.

### 34. Millennium Problems as a Stress Test for Gaps

Open problems do not simply lack proofs. Their Shadows are exceptionally compelling. Poincaré provides a contrast:

> **Shadow → Gap → Construction → Light**

Six open Millennium problems remain at the level:

> **Shadow → Gap → ?**

This does not mean they are unsolvable. It means that the gap has not yet been identified precisely enough to be approached by a construction.

### 35. Psychological Closure vs. Logical Closure

`10¹³` verified cases may psychologically resemble:

```
∀x
```

But:

```
10¹³ < ∞
```

> **psychological closure ≠ logical closure**

### 36. Universalisation Test

For a claim `C`:

```
S(C) = Shadow, G(C) = Gap, K(C) = candidate construction.
```

Ask:

```
S(C) ⇒? C
```

If no:

```
G(C) = C − S(C)   (conceptually)
```

Then:

```
K(C) ⇒? G(C) = 0
```

without circularity.

Only then assign LIGHT.

### 2. Generation and Resistance: The Dynamics of R and C

**Porshnev (1966/1979).** Boris Porshnev's *Social Psychology and History* identifies **suggestion** and **counter-suggestion** as the two fundamental forces of social systems. Suggestion spreads influence and generates social fields; counter-suggestion creates barriers, individuality, and resistance. The system is alive precisely when both forces are present. Neither total submission nor total rupture is stable.

Structural parallel: Porshnev's suggestion ≈ `∇_Δ R`; counter-suggestion ≈ `−∇_G C`. His central insight — that resistance is not a system defect but a structural component — directly anticipates §6 and §8 of TGS.

Gap: Porshnev did not isolate the invariant (`I`) as an explicit variable, did not transfer the mechanism to epistemology, and did not propose an architecture for adaptive switching between modes.

**Vygotsky (1934).** Lev Vygotsky's *Thought and Language* and his concept of the **Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)** model development as unfolding through social interaction, mediated by signs. The ZPD is the space between actual and potential development — not a deficit but a space of growth.

Structural parallel: ZPD ≈ Gap (§20). Interiorisation (external social action becoming internal psychological process) ≈ Self-Unfolding (§2). The sign as a tool that transforms cognition without destroying its function ≈ change of basis (§1).

Gap: Vygotsky's system is asymmetric toward `R` (development, expansion). It lacks an explicit epistemic constraint function (OPTICS) and does not formulate what exactly is invariant under the change of basis.

**Friston (2005, 2010).** Karl Friston's **Free Energy Principle** and Active Inference model cognition as minimisation of variational free energy. The system switches between exploration (information gain) and exploitation (prediction error minimisation) based on a quantitative criterion (expected free energy).

Structural parallel: Active Inference provides a mathematical basis for mode switching that TGS seeks. The trigger function `T = w_F·F + w_S·S + w_C·C + w_R·R + w_U·U` (§22) is an ad hoc version of what Friston derives from a variational principle.

Gap: Active Inference operates at the neural/sensorimotor level. Scaling to linguistic reasoning and epistemic modes remains open. Friston's exploration/exploitation is a single spectrum; TGS distinguishes VIEW (expansion of meaning) and OPTICS (constraint of justification) as qualitatively different operations.

### 3. The Invariant: What Survives Transformation

**Ilyenkov (1960, 1979).** Evald Ilyenkov's *Dialectics of the Abstract and the Concrete in Marx's Capital* develops the method of **ascent from the abstract to the concrete**. Thought unfolds from an initial contradiction (the "cell" — e.g., the commodity) into a full system. His concept of the **ideal** is the closest Soviet-era analogue to the TGS invariant: the ideal is not "in the head" but in the structure of activity. It survives changes of material carrier (gold → paper → digital).

Structural parallel: Ilyenkov's ideal ≈ `I` (invariant). His insistence that contradiction is the engine of development, not a logical error ≈ §8–9 of TGS (duality as information, paradox as governing object). The ascent from abstract to concrete ≈ Self-Unfolding.

Gap: Ilyenkov described a method of thought but did not propose a cognitive architecture implementing it. No analogue of LIVE/VIEW/OPTICS, no machine-readable epistemic status, no reflexive level.

**Ilyin (1918, 1953).** Ivan Ilyin's *Philosophy of Hegel as a Doctrine of the Concreteness of God and Man* and *Axioms of Religious Experience* develop the concept of the **living act of cognition**. Ilyin distinguishes **reason** (rassudok — formal-logical analysis) from **understanding** (razum — living holistic vision) and argues that the constraint on thought is not formal proof but **evidence** (ochevidnost') — a living self-evidence that stops arbitrary generation.

Structural parallel: Ilyin's reason ≈ OPTICS; understanding ≈ VIEW. His concept of evidence as constraint ≈ `−∇_G C` in the epistemic register. His God as the invariant of wholeness ≈ §7 of TGS.

Gap: Ilyin's framework is hierarchical (understanding above reason), not an equilibrium. TGS insists both poles are necessary (§6). No mechanism for adaptive switching; no reflexive level.

**Lawvere (1963); Spivak (2014); Fong & Spivak (2019).** Category theory provides the natural mathematical language for invariants. A functor `F: C → D` transfers structure between contexts. A natural transformation is what remains "coherent" under change of context. A limit is a universal property independent of specific representation.

Structural parallel: "Invariant = what survives change of basis" is literally the definition of a natural transformation. "Change of basis" is a functor. "Structural unity of the world" is a claim about the existence of a limit in a suitable category.

Gap: TGS does not use the categorical apparatus. This is the largest unresolved gap between TGS's verbal precision and its formal emptiness. Category theory is not a precursor but a **candidate formalisation** of TGS's core concepts.

### 4. Epistemic Logic and the Structure of Knowledge

**Hintikka (1962).** *Knowledge and Belief* establishes modal epistemic logic with operator `K_a φ` ("agent a knows that φ") and axioms including positive and negative introspection.

**Stalnaker (1984).** *Inquiry* models reasoning as successive narrowing of the space of possible worlds. Knowledge is not a store of facts but a partition structure.

**van Benthem (2011).** *Logical Dynamics of Information and Interaction* extends epistemic logic to dynamic operations: knowledge updates, public announcements, private messages. Knowledge is a process, not a state.

**Brandom (1994).** *Making It Explicit* argues that epistemic status is determined by inferential role, not by probability. What matters is what follows from a claim and what it follows from.

Contact with TGS: TGS's mode switching (LIVE/VIEW/OPTICS) is structurally close to van Benthem's dynamic updates. Brandom's inferentialism is close to TGS's epistemic typing: the status of a claim depends on its role in reasoning, not on a confidence score.

Divergence: Standard epistemic logic uses binary `K` (knows/doesn't know) or probabilistic degrees. TGS introduces **type** of epistemic object (`HYPOTHESIS`, `CONSTRUCTION`, `CIRCULARITY`, `METAPHYSICS`) as a separate variable not reducible to degree of belief.

TGS contribution: **MODE_ERROR** — a category of error absent from standard epistemic logic. In Hintikka's framework, error is false knowledge (`K_a φ` when `¬φ`). In TGS, error can be in the **mode**: `φ` may be true, but the legitimation regime applied to it may be wrong (§17, §49).

### 5. Philosophy of Science: Paradigms and Research Programmes

**Kuhn (1962).** *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* models paradigm shifts as changes of descriptive basis. Normal science operates within a paradigm; anomalies accumulate; crisis; revolution; new basis.

**Lakatos (1970).** "Falsification and the Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes" distinguishes the **hard core** (invariant) from the **protective belt** (auxiliary hypotheses). A progressive programme predicts novel facts; a degenerative one adjusts to old ones.

**Quine (1951, 1960).** "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" and *Word and Object* argue for ontological relativity. No facts are theory-independent. Change of ontology is change of language, not discovery of "true" structure.

Contact with TGS: TGS is essentially **Lakatos + Kuhn transferred to AI architecture**. The TGS invariant (§2) is Lakatos's hard core. Change of basis is Kuhn's paradigm shift. The difference is that TGS applies these ideas not to the history of science but to the **real-time operation of a cognitive system**.

Gap: Kuhn and Lakatos describe collective processes over decades. TGS describes switching within a single agent in seconds. This downward scaling is non-trivial and unvalidated.

### 6. Calibration and Uncertainty in AI

**Dawid (1982).** "The Well-Calibrated Bayesian" — classical definition of calibration.

**Guo et al. (ICML 2017).** "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks" — modern deep networks are systematically overconfident. Introduced ECE (Expected Calibration Error).

**Kadavath et al. (Anthropic, 2022).** "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know" — LLMs can predict correctness of their own answers above chance using `P(True)` format. Internal epistemic signal exists but is imperfect.

**Burns et al. (ICLR 2023).** "Discovering Latent Knowledge Without Supervision" — detecting "hidden knowledge" through internal activations. A model may "know" an answer but generate a different one.

**Farquhar et al. (Nature, 2024).** "Detecting hallucinations using semantic entropy" — confabulation detection through variance of semantically equivalent generations. Behavioural signal, not self-report.

Contact with TGS: TGS agrees with Kadavath et al.'s core finding — internal epistemic signal exists. The Runtime Controller (§21) is an attempt to use this signal for mode switching.

Divergence: Calibration literature operates at the level of **individual claims** (this answer is correct with probability `p`). TGS operates at the level of **system operating mode** (does this situation require verification right now?). Different levels of abstraction.

TGS contribution: Explicit separation of **mode** and **status** (§14). A system can be in OPTICS mode with `UNVERIFIED` status. Activation of checking ≠ result of checking. Calibration literature typically does not separate these variables.

### 7. Iterative Reasoning and Self-Correction

**Wei et al. (NeurIPS 2022).** Chain-of-Thought — verbalising intermediate steps improves reasoning.

**Wang et al. (ICLR 2023).** Self-Consistency — multiple reasoning paths + majority vote.

**Yao et al. (NeurIPS 2023).** Tree of Thoughts — search over reasoning alternatives with backtracking.

**Madaan et al. (NeurIPS 2023).** Self-Refine — model critiques and rewrites its own output.

**Shinn et al. (NeurIPS 2023).** Reflexion — verbal reinforcement learning from past failures.

**Lightman et al. (2023).** Process Reward Models — evaluating each reasoning step, not just the final answer.

Contact with TGS: VIEW ≈ Tree of Thoughts (expansion). OPTICS ≈ Self-Refine and PRM (constraint). The LIVE → VIEW → OPTICS → LIVE cycle generalises iterative reasoning.

Divergence: All listed approaches use the **same model** for generation and critique. TGS (§33) emphasises this as a systematic weakness: the model reproduces its own blind spots. TGS proposes **architectural separation** of generator and verifier, not just prompt-level separation.

TGS contribution: Typology of epistemic paths (Shadow / Construction / Circularity / Speculative). Self-Consistency votes on the answer; TGS classifies the **type of argumentation**.

### 8. Formal Verification of Reasoning

**Coq** (1989–), **Isabelle** (1986–), **Lean** (2015–), **Lean 4** (2021–). Shared principle: separation of **tactics** (step generation) and **kernel** (verification). The kernel is minimal and checks every step. The goal state is an explicit object representing the unclosed gap between current state and target.

**AlphaProof / AlphaGeometry** (DeepMind, 2024). Neural network proposes steps; Lean verifies. IMO 2024 silver medal.

Contact with TGS: This is the most direct realisation of Generator ∌ Verdict (§18). Lean's goal state is a machine-readable `unresolved_gap` (§20). Circularity is prevented by kernel architecture, not user discipline.

Divergence: Proof assistants work only in **fully formalisable domains**. TGS targets open-domain natural language reasoning where no formal kernel is available. Not competition but different scope.

TGS contribution: Generalisation of gap-first reasoning beyond formal mathematics. In Lean, a gap is a specific subgoal. In TGS, a gap can be "causal mechanism not independently established" — informal but explicitly represented.

### 9. Multi-Agent Verification and Debate

**Irving, Christiano, Amodei (2018).** "AI Safety via Debate" — two agents argue, a judge decides. Hypothesis: in equilibrium, lies lose because they are easier to attack than defend.

**Du et al. (ICML 2023); Liang et al. (2023).** Empirical multi-agent debate implementations.

**Bai et al. (Anthropic, 2022).** Constitutional AI / RLAIF — model critiques its own outputs against explicit principles, then trains on corrected versions.

Contact with TGS: Constitutional AI realises the Constraint Filter (§44) through explicit principles. Debate realises Resistance → diagnostic signal (§96).

Divergence: Debate approaches typically do not type the **epistemic status** of arguments. Agents argue but do not label their claims as `SHADOW`, `CONSTRUCTION`, or `CIRCULARITY`.

TGS contribution: **Anti-Oracle** (§30, §42) — a system that does not choose a debate winner but classifies all candidate paths with explicit commitments and remaining gaps. Different interface to the same process.

### 10. Mechanistic Interpretability

**Elhage et al. (Anthropic, 2021).** "A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits" — decomposing transformer behaviour into interpretable circuits.

**Cunningham et al. (2023).** Sparse autoencoders for monosemantic feature extraction.

**Templeton et al. (Anthropic, 2024).** "Scaling Monosemanticity" — extracting interpretable features from production models, including deception and sycophancy features.

Contact with TGS: TGS acknowledges the performative self-report problem (§65). Mechanistic interpretability bypasses self-report through direct observation of internal states — what TGS calls Activation Probes (§63).

Divergence: Interpretability operates at the level of **activations and features**; TGS operates at the level of **semantic structures and epistemic statuses**. Interpretability can detect that a model "thinks about deception" but cannot classify a claim as `SHADOW` or `CONSTRUCTION`.

TGS contribution: Epistemic semantics on top of mechanistic observations. If interpretability detects a sycophancy feature, TGS asks: which epistemic mode is active? Which unresolved gap is hidden?

### 11. Selective Prediction and Metacognition

**Geifman & El-Yaniv (NeurIPS 2017).** Selective classification — the model can abstain. Optimises coverage vs. accuracy trade-off.

**Jospin et al. (2022).** Survey of uncertainty estimation in neural networks: MC Dropout, ensembles, variational inference.

Contact with TGS: Selective prediction is a simplified LIVE/OPTICS. The model either answers (LIVE) or abstains (OPTICS without verification capability).

Divergence: Selective prediction is binary (answer / abstain). TGS is ternary (LIVE / VIEW / OPTICS) and includes a **generative mode** (VIEW) that is neither answer nor refusal but expansion of the space.

### 13. Honest Assessment of Novelty

TGS does not exist in a vacuum. Almost every element has precursors. This is normal and expected for a synthesis.

**Genuinely new combinations in TGS:**

1. **MODE_ERROR as an independent epistemic failure category.** No direct analogue found in the surveyed literature. Standard systems check "is the claim correct?"; TGS checks "is the right regime applied to this object?"

2. **Shadow → Gap → Construction → Light as an explicit typology for open-domain reasoning.** Formally exists in proof assistants; the transfer to natural language with machine-readable schema (§40) is new.

3. **Reflexive Optics Refinement (Part V).** Applying the epistemic grammar to the boundaries of the grammar itself, with a validation gate. Meta-learning adjusts parameters; TGS revises structure.

4. **Synthesis of Soviet psychological-philosophical tradition (Porshnev, Vygotsky, Ilyenkov) with AI epistemic architecture.** These two traditions do not typically interact. TGS bridges them.

**What TGS should acknowledge:** it stands on the shoulders of proof assistants (generator/verdict), active inference (mode switching), calibration (uncertainty), Lakatos (invariant), Porshnev (resistance as structure), Vygotsky (gap as space), and Ilyenkov (contradiction as engine). Its contribution is not the invention of these ideas but their unification and the addition of several new categories.
